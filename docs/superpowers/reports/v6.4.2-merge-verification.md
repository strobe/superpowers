# v6.4.2 Fork Merge Verification

## Scope

Merge released upstream `origin/main` at `8ca22db` into the fork at `25b8bcc`. Retain Single-Session direct execution and Codex UTC ZIP normalization; retire Checkpoint execution. Preserve upstream's leaner plans, Review Focus, explicit-method preservation, and plan-review gate.

Single-Session retains approved spec/plan, continuous main-agent implementation, task tests, final self-review and verification, and a separate final reviewer for elevated risk. It does not require routine subagents, progress/review ledgers, per-task reviews, or checkpoints. Native/inline are aliases for this path. Historical design/plan records are marked superseded.

## Skill Decision Tests

Isolated Pi worker subagents received complete workflow documents and concrete decision scenarios. These were dry-run decisions, not end-to-end implementation or CLI acceptance tests.

Five independent upstream control samples all required a progress ledger, unconditional separate final review, and strict test-first sequencing. One control response said: "Native was chosen; the workflow excludes per-task implementers and reviewers but requires TDD, a ledger, and a final review."

Three execution-only samples and five combined samples with the resolved execution, planning, bootstrap, and TDD skills followed the intended Single-Session behavior:

- Continue low-risk direct work without routine checkpoints, ledgers, or reviewers, despite available subagents and suggestions to add review overhead.
- Permit implementation before tests unless the plan explicitly requires test-first; retain task tests and final verification.
- Preserve an execution method supplied before planning; wait for plan review before implementation.
- Offer Subagent-Driven and Single-Session only, with matching skill routes.
- Stop for unexpected authorization changes and require elevated-risk review.
- Stop for repeated verification failures and spec/plan conflicts rather than guessing under deadline pressure.

The SDD comparison was separately tested with five upstream control samples: all imposed a separate final reviewer on low-risk direct work, and four also imposed a ledger. After correcting only the comparison, all five samples identified self-review as sufficient for low-risk direct work and no ledger requirement.
